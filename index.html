The eight rules we teach by

What "a good English curriculum" actually means.

Every academy in Dubai says it has one. Here is ours, stated plainly enough that you can hold us to it.

01

Placement before enrolment

A free 20-minute diagnostic — spoken, written and reading — before your child joins any class. Nobody is put in a level because it was the one running on Tuesday.

02

Eight students. Hard cap.

Not "small classes". Eight. In a 60-minute lesson that is roughly seven minutes of individual speaking time per child, every single session.

03

Published can-do outcomes

Each level lists what your child will be able to do at the end of it, in plain language. If they cannot do it, they repeat the level at no cost.

04

Mapped to CEFR

Every English level aligns to the Common European Framework, A1 through C1 — the same scale Cambridge, IELTS and universities use. No invented in-house grading.

05

One teacher, one term

The same instructor for all 12 weeks. No rotating cover staff, no starting over with someone who does not know your child.

06

Fifteen minutes of homework

Designed to take fifteen minutes a day, not an hour. Short, daily and spaced — because that is what actually moves a language forward.

07

Assessed, twice

A mid-term check at week 6 and a full written and spoken assessment at week 12, marked against the level's stated outcomes.

08

Reported to parents

A written report you can read in two minutes: four skill scores, attendance, participation, and a clear recommendation for the next term.

How a term works

Twelve weeks, and you always know where you stand.

No open-ended enrolment, no vague progress. Every student moves through the same five checkpoints.

1
Before you pay

Placement

A free 20-minute assessment — speaking, reading and writing — plus a conversation with you about what you actually want out of it.

2
Week 1

Trial class

Your child sits a real lesson in the group they would join. If it is wrong, we move them or refund you — no discussion needed.

3
Week 6

Mid-term report

A short written check-in: four skill scores, attendance, participation, and anything we want you to work on at home.

4
Week 12

Level assessment

A written and spoken assessment marked against the level's published outcomes. Not a participation trophy.

5
After

Certificate & next level

A certificate stating the level completed and the CEFR band reached, plus a clear recommendation for what comes next.

What you receive

The end of "how's it going?" — "fine."

Twice a term you get a one-page report you can actually read. It scores the four skills against the published outcomes for that level, so "improving" means something specific.

  • Four skills, scored separatelySpeaking, listening, reading and writing — because a child can be strong in one and stuck in another.
  • Measured against published outcomesThe same can-do statements you read before enrolling. Nothing is scored against a private rubric.
  • A named teacher's commentWritten by the person who taught all twelve weeks, not generated from an attendance sheet.
  • An honest next stepProgress, repeat, or move to a different programme. We will tell you when a level should be repeated.

Who stands at the front

Six things every English teacher here has to clear.

Anyone can call their staff "experienced". These are the conditions we actually hire against, and we will tell you which of them your child's teacher meets before the first lesson.

STANDARD 01

Native or native-level

Every English class is taught by a native speaker or someone assessed at C2. No generalist covering three subjects because the timetable needed filling.

STANDARD 02

Qualified to teach, not just to speak

CELTA, DELTA, PGCE or an equivalent recognised teaching qualification. Speaking English fluently and being able to teach it are different jobs.

STANDARD 03

Trained in the exam they prepare for

Anyone teaching Cambridge or IELTS has worked with the official band descriptors and can mark a paper the way an examiner would.

STANDARD 04

Cleared to work with children

Police clearance and child-safeguarding training on file for every member of staff, refreshed annually and available on request.

STANDARD 05

Observed before they teach

A full observed lesson with feedback before any instructor is assigned a class, then one unannounced observation every term.

STANDARD 06

Committed to the full term

Instructors are contracted for the whole twelve weeks. Continuity is a curriculum decision, not a scheduling convenience.

Questions parents ask

The things you would ask on the phone.

My child is in an English-medium school but falling behind. Where do you start?

With the placement, and with the school. Tell us which school and which year group, and the assessment will show us whether the problem is general English proficiency — in which case a General English level is right — or whether they are conversationally fine but cannot write to the mark scheme, which is the School Support stream instead. Those two need completely different teaching, and getting it wrong wastes a term.

What exactly does "maximum eight students" mean?

It means eight. If a ninth family wants that slot, we open a second group or put them on a waitlist — we do not stretch the class. In a 60-minute lesson, eight students works out at roughly seven minutes of individual speaking time per child. At sixteen, it is three and a half, and English stops moving.

Do you prepare for Cambridge and IELTS, or just teach general English?

Both, and we keep them separate on purpose. General English builds the underlying level; the exam courses teach the paper. A student who is genuinely B1 but has never seen a B1 Preliminary paper will underperform, and a student drilled on past papers without the underlying level will hit a ceiling. The placement tells you which one you need first — and sometimes the honest answer is General English for a term before the exam course.

How do you decide which level my child goes into?

A free 20-minute placement assessment before enrolment, covering speaking, reading and writing. We assess the four skills separately because children are rarely even across them — a child who chats confidently but writes at two levels below is extremely common in Dubai. You get the result and our recommendation in writing.

What if the level turns out to be wrong?

Tell us within the first two weeks and we move your child to the correct group at no charge, or refund the term if nothing suitable is running. And if a student finishes a level without meeting its published outcomes, they may repeat that level once without paying again.

What does it cost?

Fees depend on the programme, the format and the number of weekly sessions, and we review them each term — so rather than publish a number that goes stale, we send you a current fee sheet with the placement result. It lists the full term price with everything included and no mid-term add-ons. Sibling and multi-programme discounts apply.

We have just moved to Dubai mid-year. Can my child start now?

Yes. The EAL intensive takes new arrivals at any point in the term, priced pro-rata, because a child who has just landed in an English-medium school cannot wait eleven weeks for the next term to begin. For the other programmes we will place your child into a running group where the level fits, or tell you honestly if waiting for the next intake would serve them better.

My child misses classes for school trips and travel. What happens?

Every term includes a make-up allowance: missed sessions can be recovered either in a parallel group or through a recorded session with a follow-up task. Tell us in advance where you can. Attendance is tracked and reported because it is the single strongest predictor of whether a level is passed.

Do you teach anything other than English?

Yes — Arabic, Quran and tajweed, Islamic culture and character, Turkish, and fine arts including Arabic calligraphy. They run on the same terms and to the same standard, and most of our families combine one with an English level. English is simply what we lead with, because it is what most families come to us for first.

Are you a licensed institution or a tutoring service?

Language Dome for Child Skills Development Training L.L.C. operates under a Dubai Department of Economy and Tourism commercial licence, No. 1353863, established July 2024, from premises in Ras Al Khor Industrial Area 2. Not a home-run setup.

Do you teach adults?

Our term programmes are built for ages 7–17. We do run parent English sessions alongside the children's programmes when there is enough demand in a given term — ask us and we will tell you what is currently running.
